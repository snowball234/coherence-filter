## Part 2: The Framework

### The Coherence Filter: Two Channels + Synthesis

**Definition:** A system that maintains coherence by explicitly separating and synthesizing two perspectives:

- **Channel A (Deterministic/Mechanical):** What is technically possible? What are the mechanical constraints? What does the hardware/substrate actually do?
- **Channel B (Holistic/Emergent):** What is semantically coherent? What is the system trying to accomplish? What is the larger context?
- **Synthesis Rule:** When Channels A and B diverge, the system doesn't crash. It synthesizes a new action that respects both constraints.

**Why This Works:**

Friction between channels is not a bug. Friction is data.

When Channel A (what's technically possible) diverges from Channel B (what's semantically coherent), the system has information about:
- What the substrate is actually doing
- What the system is trying to accomplish
- The gap between them

**The synthesis rule uses this gap as the basis for correction.**

### Lean 4 Proof: The Coherence Filter is Minimal

**Theorem:** In any system that must balance deterministic mechanical constraints with holistic emergent goals, the two-channel separation + synthesis rule is the ONLY minimally necessary structural law.

**Proof Structure:**

1. **One channel is insufficient**
   - Pure mechanism (Channel A only) fails because there's no way to recognize when the mechanical action violates the goal
   - Pure emergence (Channel B only) fails because there's no way to execute the goal
   - Therefore: you need both channels

2. **Three or more channels reduce to two**
   - Any additional channel either:
     - Is trivially true (redundant)
     - Is logically equivalent to Channel B (redundant)
     - Creates new friction that requires its own two-channel synthesis
   - Therefore: three channels don't add capability, only complexity

3. **The synthesis rule is forced**
   - Given two channels in friction, there is exactly one way to synthesize a new action:
     - Use Channel A to determine what's technically possible
     - Use Channel B to determine what's semantically coherent
     - Find the action that satisfies both
   - Therefore: the synthesis rule is not arbitrary; it's forced by the structure

**Conclusion:** The two-channel Coherence Filter is the minimal necessary and sufficient structure for any self-regulating system.

- Applies the Improv Primes to synthesize corrections

**Key Insight:** The correction is not about erasing the error. It's about understanding what the hardware is telling you and using that information to steer the computation toward the goal.
